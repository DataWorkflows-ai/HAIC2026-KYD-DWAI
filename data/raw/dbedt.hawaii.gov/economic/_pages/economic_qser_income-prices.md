## B. Income & Prices

3rd Quarter 2026 Report
Download Income Data Tables (spreadsheet)
- [Download Income Data Tables (spreadsheet)](https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/economic/data_reports/qser/B-income-tables.xlsx)
In the first quarter of 2026, total annualized nominal GDP increased $6,153 million or 5.0 percent, from the same quarter of 2025. In 2025, total annualized nominal GDP increased $6,980 million or 5.9 percent from the previous year. In the first quarter of 2026, total annualized real GDP (in chained 2017 dollars) increased $1,250 million or 1.3 percent from the same quarter of 2025. In 2025, total annualized real GDP increased $2,266 million or 2.5 percent from the previous year (Tables B-1 to B-3).
Hawai‘i’s total personal income increased during the first quarter of 2026, over the same quarter of 2025; all components of personal income increased over the same quarter of the previous year, except state unemployment insurance compensation and other personal current transfer receipts.
In the first quarter of 2026, total nominal annualized personal income (i.e. not adjusted for inflation) increased $2,904.8 million or 2.7 percent over that of 2025 (Table B-5). In 2025, average personal income was $109,903.7 million, an increase of $7,199.9 million or 7.0 percent from the previous year. In the first quarter of 2026, personal income per capita was $76,797, a 3.0 percent increase over the same quarter of the previous year (Table B-6).
In the first quarter of 2026, wages and salaries increased $998.6 million or 2.0 percent over the same quarter of 2025. In 2025, wages and salaries increased $2,507.2 million or 5.2 percent from the previous year (Table B-8).
Supplements to wages and salaries, consisting of employer payments to retirement plans, private group health insurance plans, private workers compensation plans, and other such benefits, increased $416.0 million or 2.9 percent in the first quarter of 2026 from the same quarter of 2025 (Table B-9). In 2025, supplements to wages and salaries increased $856.2 million or 6.2 percent from the previous year.
Proprietors’ income increased $168.0 million or 2.0 percent in the first quarter of 2026 over that of 2025 (Table B-10). In 2025, proprietors’ income was up $305.4 million or 3.8 percent from the previous year.
Dividends, interest, and rent increased $456.9 million or 2.0 percent in the first quarter of 2026 from the same quarter of 2025. In 2025, income in this category was up $677.9 million or 3.0 percent from the previous year (Table B-11).
The annualized personal current transfer receipts increased $1,124.3 million or 6.0 percent in the first quarter of 2026 from the same quarter of 2025 (Table B-12). In 2025, personal current transfer receipts increased $3,330.1 million or 18.4 percent from the previous year. The large increase in transfer receipts in 2025 reflects a settlement paid by an electric utility to households to compensate for losses related to the 2023 Maui wildfire.
Contributions to government social insurance (which is subtracted from total personal income) increased $259.0 million or 2.9 percent in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same quarter of 2025. In 2025, these contributions increased $476.8 million or 5.7 percent from the previous year (Table B-13).
In the first quarter of 2026, total non-farm private sector annualized earnings increased $1,549.6 million or 2.1 percent from the same quarter of 2025 (Table B-4). In dollar terms, the largest increase occurred in Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services; followed by Healthcare and Social Assistance; and Accommodation and Food Service. During the first quarter of 2026, total government earnings decreased $4.8 million or less than 0.1 percent from the same quarter of 2025. Earnings from the Federal Government increased $413.0 million, due to increases in earnings in the military component. Earnings from the State and Local Governments decreased $417.8 million in the quarter.
In the first half of 2026, Honolulu’s Consumer Price Index for Urban Consumers (CPI-U) increased 4.0 percent from the same period in 2025 (Table B-14). This is 0.7 percentage points above the 3.3 percent increase for the U.S. average CPI-U in the first half of 2026. It is higher than the 2.9 percent increase in the Honolulu CPI-U for the first half of 2025 compared to the same period of the previous year. In the first half of 2026, the Honolulu CPI-U increased the most in Apparel (8.9 percent), followed by Medical Care (6.0 percent), Housing (4.7 percent), Recreation (4.3 percent), Food and Beverages (3.0 percent), Transportation (2.8 percent), Other Goods and Services (0.9 percent); and Education and Communication (0.5 percent).
