## State of the Economy

3rd Quarter 2026 Report
Download Data Table (spreadsheet)
- [Download Data Table (spreadsheet)](https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/economic/data_reports/qser/4-state-economy.xlsx)
Hawai‘i’s major economic indicators were mixed in the second quarter of 2026. The civilian labor force and employment, wage and salary jobs, tax revenues, and visitor arrivals increased. However, government contracts awarded and private building permit authorizations decreased.
The total number of visitor arrivals by air increased 22,985 or 0.9 percent in the second quarter of 2026, compared to the second quarter of 2025. The total average daily census decreased by 20,388 or 8.9 percent in the quarter.
In the second quarter of 2026, the construction sector added 1,300 jobs or 3.2 percent compared with the same quarter of 2025, while the permit value for private construction decreased $432.3 million or 27.8 percent. Government contracts awarded in the second quarter of 2026 decreased $186.9 million or 25.4 percent compared to the same quarter of 2025. According to the most recent data available, the contracting tax base increased 0.3 percent or $11.2 million in the first quarter of 2026, compared to the same quarter of the previous year.
In the second quarter of 2026, the state general excise tax revenue increased $22.8 million or 1.9 percent, the transient accommodations tax (TAT) increased $21.7 million or 10.7 percent, the net corporate income tax revenues increased $45.0 million or 20.5 percent, and the net individual income tax revenues decreased $3.0 million or 0.3 percent.
Hawai‘i’s labor market conditions were mixed in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the same quarter of 2025. The civilian labor force and civilian employment increased modestly. Civilian unemployment and the unemployment rate also increased. Civilian non-agricultural wage and salary jobs increased in the private sector but decreased in the federal government sector compared to the second quarter of 2025.
The private sector added about 4,400 non-agricultural jobs compared to second quarter of 2025. The number of jobs increased the most in Health Care and Social Assistance, which gained 2,700 jobs or 3.5 percent; followed by Construction, which added 1,300 jobs or 3.2 percent; Accommodations, which gained 800 jobs or 2.0 percent; Food Service and Drinking Places, which gained 600 jobs or 0.9 percent; and Information which added 300 jobs or 4.5 percent in the quarter. The Government sector lost 1,700 jobs or 1.3 percent in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the same quarter of 2025. The decline was in the Federal Sector, which lost 2,900 jobs or 8.2 percent. On the other hand, the State Government added 500 jobs or 0.7 percent and the Local Government added 700 jobs or 3.6 percent, compared to the second quarter of 2025.
In the first quarter of 2026, total nominal annualized personal income (i.e. not adjusted for inflation) increased $2,904.8 million or 2.7 percent over that of 2025. In 2025, average personal income was $109,903.7 million, an increase of $7,199.9 million or 7.0 percent from the previous year. In the first quarter of 2026, personal income per capita was $76,797, a 3.0 percent increase over the same quarter of the previous year.
In the first quarter of 2026, total non-farm private sector annualized earnings increased $1,549.6 million or 2.1 percent from the same quarter of 2025. In dollar terms, the largest increase occurred in Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services; followed by Health Care and Social Assistance; and Accommodation and Food Service. During the first quarter of 2026, total government earnings decreased $4.8 million or less than 0.1 percent from the same quarter of 2025. Earnings from the Federal Government increased $413.0 million. Earnings from the State and Local Governments decreased $417.8 million in the quarter.
In the first half of 2026, Honolulu’s Consumer Price Index for Urban Consumers (CPI-U) increased 4.0 percent from the same period in 2025. This is 0.7 percentage points above the 3.3 percent increase for the U.S. average CPI-U in the first half of 2026. It is higher than the 2.9 percent increase in the Honolulu CPI-U for the first half of 2025 compared to the same period of the previous year. In the first half of 2026, the Honolulu CPI-U increased the most in Apparel (8.9 percent), followed by Medical Care (6.0 percent), Housing (4.7 percent), Recreation (4.3 percent), Food and Beverages (3.0 percent), Transportation (2.8 percent), Other Goods and Services (0.9 percent); and Education and Communication (0.5 percent).
