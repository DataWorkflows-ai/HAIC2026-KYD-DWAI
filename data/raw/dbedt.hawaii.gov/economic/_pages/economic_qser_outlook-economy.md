## Outlook for the Economy

3rd Quarter 2026 Report
Download Data Table (spreadsheet)
- [Download Data Table (spreadsheet)](https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/economic/data_reports/qser/5-outlook.xlsx)
The construction industry saw mixed performance through July 2026 as new activity came under pressure while ongoing projects continued to support job growth. The average construction payroll job count was 41,200 during the first seven months of 2026, a 3.3 percent increase compared to the same period last year. Meanwhile, the value of private building permits decreased 34.0 percent during the first seven months of 2026. Government contracts also decreased, by 25.4 percent year-over-year in the first half of 2026.
Hawai‘i’s labor market has been mixed in recent months. The civilian labor force and non-agricultural wage and salary jobs expanded slightly while the unemployment rate increased. Non-agricultural wage and salary jobs rose by 0.2 percent in the first seven months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 and the labor force grew 0.2 percent (not seasonally adjusted). Job losses in the federal sector were slightly offset by job growth in the private sector. In July, the State had the seventh lowest unemployment rate at 2.9 percent (not seasonally adjusted) in the nation.
State general excise tax collections increased by 3.3 percent in the first seven months of 2026 (latest data available) compared to the same period a year ago.
Inflation, as measured by the Honolulu Consumer Price Index for Urban Consumers (CPI-U) was 5.6 percent higher in July 2026 than a year earlier. This is an acceleration from the beginning of 2026 when January inflation was 2.4 percent. The most important components of the Honolulu CPI-U – Housing, Food and Beverages, and Transportation saw year-over-year increases in July of 6.1 percent, 3.6 percent, and 6.4 percent, respectively. Honolulu inflation was above the U.S. as a whole, where prices rose 3.4 percent between July 2025 and July 2026.
At the national level, the most recent (August 2026) Blue Chip Economic Indicators report, the consensus of 50 economic forecasting organizations, projected the U.S. economy to grow at 2.1 percent in 2026, and 2.0 percent in 2027. DBEDT estimates that Hawai‘i’s real GDP will increase by 1.3 percent in 2026, 1.6 percent in 2027, and 1.8 percent in both 2028 and 2029.
Visitor arrivals are projected to grow 0.9 percent in 2026 and then increase 1.0 percent per year between 2027 and 2029 Visitor spending is projected to be $22.4 billion in 2026 and is expected to increase to $25.0 billion by 2029.
Non-agriculture payroll jobs are expected to increase by 0.3 percent in 2026 and then by 0.5 percent each year between 2027 and 2029.
The state unemployment rate is expected to be 2.5 percent in 2026 and 2027 and will improve to 2.4 percent in 2028 and 2.3 percent 202Personal income is expected to grow at 4.0 percent in 2026, 4.2 percent in 2027 and 2028, and 4.1 percent in 2029.
As measured by the Honolulu Consumer Price Index for Urban Consumers, inflation is expected to be at 4.4 percent in 2026, which is higher than the projected U.S. consumer inflation rate of 3.4 percent for the same year. Hawai‘i consumer inflation is expected to decrease to 2.5 percent by 2029.
Hawai‘i’s population growth is expected to be flat in 2026 and then increase by 0.1 percent each year between 2027 and 2029.
